# Single-shot camera and continuity grammar

## One-shot contract

Use exactly `[Shot 1]`. Do not create cuts, hidden cuts, scene transitions, or a second viewpoint disguised by full-frame effects. Keep one continuous spatial and temporal path from entrance through exit.

## Camera job

Choose one camera job:

- hold a stable product-like frame while the subject travels or changes;
- follow a moving subject with a small pan or track;
- make one limited arc or push that clarifies construction or action;
- recenter once as the subject reaches its display state.

Use one main camera behavior plus at most one small correction. Avoid large environment reveals, repeated orbits, camera roll, rapid lens changes, or movement that competes with the subject.

## Framing and safe motion

- Make the subject readable within the first 0.2-0.3 seconds.
- Give the entrance a defined edge, depth, size, direction, and speed.
- Keep the defining silhouette and core object clear during the main beat.
- Leave enough room along the intended exit path.
- Use 1:1 or 4:3 composition only. Default to 1:1.
- Keep action inside a compact central-safe region during the display phase while allowing entry and exit to cross the frame edge.

## Solid-background camera behavior

When a solid background is required, keep it visually stationary and uniform across the frame. Camera motion may follow or arc around the subject only if the field remains one unchanged color without perspective cues, gradients, or environmental parallax. Describe the desired constant hue, luminance, and full-frame coverage positively.

## Continuity variables

Maintain these through the single shot:

1. subject identity, count, construction, and front/back orientation;
2. prop ownership and attachment;
3. motion direction, speed logic, and action phase;
4. trail, petal, sparkle, smoke, or material-response direction;
5. background mode and color stability;
6. the relationship between subject movement and camera follow.

## Exit completion

Use one exit family supported by the action:

- trajectory beyond an edge;
- return or curved departure;
- closure, folding, retraction, or compacting;
- subject/effect opacity decreasing smoothly to zero over the unchanged background.

Complete the exit by the final timestamp for T2VA and ordinary Ref2VA. It is valid for the final frame to contain only the stable solid background when the chosen exit removes the subject completely. For FL2VA or L2VA, let the continuous shot settle into the exact supplied last frame instead; do not force an exit that contradicts the endpoint.
