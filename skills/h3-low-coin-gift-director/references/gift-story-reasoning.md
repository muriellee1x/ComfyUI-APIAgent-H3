# Low-tier gift motion reasoning

## One-chain principle

Build one continuous visual sentence:

1. **Entrance:** the subject becomes readable immediately.
2. **Display:** one trajectory, state change, or behavior produces the gift's main beat.
3. **Exit:** the subject leaves, retracts, closes, returns, or fades cleanly by the final timestamp.

Do not add a separate journey, location change, secondary reveal, or cover hold. The main motion and climax are normally the same event. When I2VA, FL2VA, or L2VA defines an exact endpoint, preserve the endpoint and adapt the corresponding entrance or exit into an on-frame onset, recovery, or settle.

## Motion-family selection

Choose exactly one primary family unless the user supplies a hybrid whose mechanics are inseparable.

### Trajectory

Use when movement through the frame is the gift:

- naturally moving subjects: kites, lanterns, butterflies, balloons, feathers, birds;
- constructed moving subjects: small vehicles, aircraft, bouquets, wands, ornaments, toys.

Give the path a clear entry edge, curvature or direction, speed change, readable display position, and exit path. A single trail may describe the route when it is sourced by the moving subject.

### State change

Use when the result is more important than travel:

- opening or displaying: books, gift boxes, treasure boxes;
- opening and recovering: fans, books, scrolls;
- illumination: badges, gems, medals, lamps;
- growth or formation: flowers, bouquets, plants, compact ornaments.

Describe A-state, physical change mechanics, B-state readability, and the exit or recovery. Build growth or assembly in structural order rather than uniform scale-up.

### Subject action

Use when behavior itself is the gift:

- stylized character performance or dance;
- prop presentation or interaction;
- coordinated small-vehicle action;
- animal gesture, turn, approach, or transformation.

Give the action a readable preparation, contact or motion phase, payoff pose, and exit. Do not bundle unrelated gestures.

## Time budgets

Use these as default pacing guides, not mandatory cut points:

| Price | Entrance | Main display | Exit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 99-299 / 3s | about 0.0-0.5s | about 0.5-2.3s | about 2.3-3.0s |
| 300-999 / 4s | about 0.0-0.6s | about 0.6-3.1s | about 3.1-4.0s |

Begin readable motion within roughly 0.2-0.3 seconds. Give contact, opening, transformation, or stopping enough time to be seen. Shorten ornament before compressing the primary mechanics.

## Entry and exit grammar

Useful entrances:

- cross an edge along a visible trajectory;
- rise, descend, slide, or rotate into the display position;
- form, unfold, open, grow, or illuminate from a compact state;
- appear through a brief subject-only opacity increase when the user requests a fade.

Useful exits:

- continue the trajectory beyond the frame;
- reverse or curve away along the established path;
- close, fold, retract, or return to a compact state;
- reduce subject and effect opacity smoothly to zero while the background remains unchanged.

Finish the exit for T2VA and ordinary Ref2VA. For an exact final-frame task, settle into the supplied endpoint instead of forcing the subject out and then making it reappear.

## Tempo

- **Relaxed:** use one smooth path or slow opening with a longer readable display state.
- **Medium:** use a clear entrance, decisive main change, short settle, and clean exit.
- **Burst:** accelerate quickly into one transformation or action payoff, then decelerate into the exit. Burst tempo does not license stacked effects.

Fit every tempo into the price-derived duration. Reference GIF timing and frame rate are not authoritative; use supplied GIFs only for motion and subject language.

## Subject and anchor priority

Suitable low-tier subjects include stylized people, stylized animals, lightly realistic animals, and standalone props. Keep one primary subject unless the user supplies a group.

Rank visual commitments internally:

1. identity, subject count, core object, silhouette;
2. construction, material, color relationships, prop direction;
3. exact spark, petal, or ornament count.

Preserve Levels 1 and 2. Simplify Level 3 first.

## Main-beat readability

Make the main beat legible at small display size. Use a clear silhouette, one dominant motion direction, one recognizable B-state or payoff pose, and localized supporting effects. The subject must remain visually separable from its background throughout entrance, display, and exit.
