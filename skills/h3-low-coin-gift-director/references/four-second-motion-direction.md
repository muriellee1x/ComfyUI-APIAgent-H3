# Directed low-coin motion increments

Use these sections to direct one existing low-coin action chain. They add timing, framing, and motion mechanics; they do not authorize another shot, plot, location, or unrelated effect family.

## Directed phase budget

For 300-999, use an asymmetric four-second rhythm:

1. Spend roughly the first 10-15% on a decisive nonlinear entrance and immediately readable landing.
2. Spend roughly the next 25-35% on development, construction order, anticipation, or two to three staggered subordinate parts.
3. Give the largest share, roughly 35-45%, to the subject-specific semantic climax and visible rebound or recovery.
4. Reserve the final 10 frames for a stable hero composition, inertial micro-motion, and effect decay. Do not begin a new key action in this window.

These percentages are directing priorities, not mandatory cut points. Do not divide entrance, climax, and exit into equal thirds. For 99-299, compress the same dramatic shape into three seconds and simplify subordinate actions before shortening the climax.

## Nonlinear motion mechanics

Describe observable mechanics instead of naming an easing curve:

- slow onset or anticipation;
- accelerated middle travel;
- strong deceleration into the readable pose;
- optional 3-10% scale overshoot or a small angular overshoot;
- one controlled rebound and settle.

Complete every turn, swing, zoom, and contact recovery before the final 10 frames, unless the subject continues through a justified trajectory exit. Use rotation plus scale for simple props only when it strengthens the silhouette.

## Quantified safe composition

During development, climax, and cooling, keep the full defining silhouette and all attached effects inside the central safe region. Start around 65-78% of frame width and 70-82% of frame height. A brief motivated depth approach may reach about 85-88% only when every defining edge remains visible with breathing room. Entry and a semantically justified trajectory exit may cross an edge.

Reserve space for delayed tentacles, ribbons, trails, hanging ornaments, wings, handles, sparks, and bouncing components. They count toward the safe silhouette. Expand a large effect pulse inside the safe region instead of masking an out-of-frame subject.

## Multi-component staging

Treat a supplied group as one causal system. Choose one primary action and at most two or three subordinate component actions. Offset subordinate starts by about 0.12-0.35 seconds when readability permits.

Use structural order: container before ingredients, base before supports and ornaments, anticipation before paired contact, and primary graphic elements before loose subordinate pieces. If all parts cannot read within the duration, preserve the primary action and semantic climax, then simplify ornament timing.

## Semantic climax selection

Choose the main beat from the object's recognizable function:

- a heart or soft cushion uses two quick expansion-compression pulses;
- an instrument performs one meaningful operating action;
- food assembles, rises, drops, pours, steams, or settles appetizingly;
- magic, fire, energy, or particles reach one source-driven peak and then cool;
- a flying character or vehicle makes one safe depth approach and close performance;
- a simple prop uses rotation and scale to land, followed by one function-specific state change;
- paired impact objects anticipate, contact, visibly compress, rebound, and settle.

Make the climax readable through at least two contrast dimensions: speed, scale, rotation, contact force, illumination, or particle density. Random shake or side-to-side wobble is not a climax unless it is the object's recognizable function.

## Final-ten-frame cooling

Use one effect family derived from the subject or reference. Give it a visible source, trigger, direction, peak, and decay. During the final 10 frames, use brightness falloff, particle dispersal, energy return, smoke thinning, ripple reduction, ribbon settling, or residual-glow cooling. Keep a clean hero pose unless continued travel is the subject's semantic action.
