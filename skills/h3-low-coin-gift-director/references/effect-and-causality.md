# Low-tier effect and causality rules

## One-system default

Start with the subject's physical motion. Add at most one coherent supporting effect system when the reference, user, or gift mechanism justifies it. One system may contain many particles of the same family, such as a single sparkle trail or one blossom burst; do not count every particle separately.

An effect is justified when it does at least one job:

- traces the path of a moving subject;
- makes an opening, illumination, growth, or transformation readable;
- expresses contact or release from a visible source;
- belongs to the supplied visual identity.

## Source-action-result test

For every trail, glow, sparkle, petal, heart, flare, or material response, specify:

1. its visible source;
2. the subject action that produces it;
3. its direction and duration;
4. the result it clarifies;
5. how it decays or exits with the subject.

Omit an effect when those mechanics are vague. Do not invent a separate messenger object or activation beam to start the gift.

## Price calibration

- **99-299:** use no effect or one brief localized system tied directly to the main motion.
- **300-499:** allow one developed localized system or one subordinate material response; do not stack both as separate climaxes.
- **500-999:** allow one developed effect system and, when no exact background marker exists and a compact background is used, one subordinate environmental response. When an exact background marker exists, keep all responses localized to the foreground instead.

Explicit reference content may exceed these defaults after the required warning, but the animation remains one shot and one action chain.

## Solid-background containment

Below 500 coins, keep the background one uniform field using the exact task color from first frame to last. Apply the same rule at 500-999 whenever `[APIAGENT_GIFT_BG_COLOR=#RRGGBB]` is present. Describe effects as foreground elements attached to or emitted by the subject. Keep their source, depth, and decay readable while the background hue and luminance remain constant.

Repeat the exact marker value and do not introduce any other background color. Keep glows, particles, reflections, smoke, and color spill localized so they never recolor or texture the field.

## Physical examples

- A kite enters diagonally, its one fine tail tracing the curve, holds long enough to read the pattern, then follows the same path out.
- A book rotates into view, opens through the hinge, flowers rise from the pages, the pages close, and the book exits.
- A fan unfolds rib by rib, one matching ornamental line follows the edge, the fan holds briefly, then folds and retracts.
- A stylized bear enters holding one rose, performs one presentation gesture, receives one localized heart response, then turns and exits.
