# Emotion Visual Rules

Use these emotions as a visual reference framework, not as hard constraints. User input, theme logic, story, price tier, and narrative expression come first. Emotion labels help choose visual elements, atmosphere, and expression direction; they must not replace the user's theme or force unrelated elements into the story.

Prefer one dominant emotional direction when it helps the concept stay clear. If the story naturally contains more than one feeling, keep one dominant atmosphere and add only a small amount of supporting texture. Do not turn the prompt into a mixed pile of unrelated emotion symbols.

## 趣味

Reference use: light, cute, playful, childlike, toy-like, or friendly contrast themes.

Visual subjects:

- Small cats, dogs, rabbits, bears, penguins, round animals, toy-like characters, clowns, magicians, pastry characters, personified gift boxes.

Visual elements:

- Colorful gift boxes, balloons, candy, lollipops, cupcakes, carousel details, playing cards, ribbons, toy mechanisms, small hats, round mini stages.

Scene and material direction:

- Bright colors, soft candy color accents, rounded forms, small ordered objects, toy-store or dessert-shop textures, glossy paper, soft fabric, cream, sugar, jelly, painted wood.

Usage principle:

- Use playful elements only when they serve the story. Do not add a clown, candy, or toy just because the direction is `趣味`.

Avoid:

- Relying on abstract words such as `搞笑`, `整活`, or `热梗`.
- Adding many unrelated toys, stickers, or emoji-like props that break the theme.

## 彰显

Reference use: noble, high-presence, ceremonial, identity-forward, expensive, elegant, or high-value themes.

Visual subjects:

- Young lady, noble youth, elegant host, rare divine creature, high-end gift, luxury tea set, perfume, jewelry box, premium installation.

Visual elements:

- Luxury spaces, calm protagonist posture, refined service relationship, silver tea set, bone china cup, velvet tablecloth, glass tea pavilion, garden columns, crystal chandelier, jewelry box, perfume bottle, haute-couture gift, rare auspicious creature.

Scene and material direction:

- Express value through environment scale, controlled spatial order, layered materials, polished surfaces, silk, velvet, glass, silver, bone china, clear crystal, pearl, restrained gem-like refraction, carved furniture, arranged tableware, and calm lighting.
- Use laser, holographic, rainbow-reflective, or highly iridescent crystal materials only when the user explicitly asks for that look or the theme truly depends on it. Do not use laser or rainbow crystal effects as a shortcut for expensive, ceremonial, or high-value expression.

Usage principle:

- Show `彰显` through environment quality, character state, material hierarchy, service relation, spatial order, and overall atmosphere. The subject should feel naturally supported by the scene, not decorated with a blunt status label.

Avoid:

- Do not default to badges, crowns, logos, trophies, gold text, family crests, or medal-like symbols.
- Use these direct symbols only when the user explicitly requests them.

## 应援

Reference use: support, guarding, companionship, encouragement, group resonance, lighting up, or being accompanied.

Visual subjects:

- Supported main character, guardian animal, star team, flower-bouquet figures, light installation, central stage figure.

Visual elements:

- Star light, flower road, cheering sticks, light-board-like dots, enclosing wreaths, warm string lights, same-color flags, stage-center light, protective halo shapes, grouped ribbons.

Scene and material direction:

- Warm light, clear central focus, surrounding elements pointing toward the supported subject, repeated small lights or flowers forming order, soft gold, warm pink, sky blue, or coordinated team colors.

Usage principle:

- Make the relationship visible: who is being supported, who or what supports them, and how the support appears in the image.

Avoid:

- Writing only `加油`, `打call`, or slogan text.
- Stacking light boards or cheering props without a clear supported subject.

## 惊喜

Reference use: gift, reveal, discovery, romance, dreamlike unveiling, confession, or hidden content themes.

Visual subjects:

- Gift box, treasure box, bouquet, teacup, letter, hidden space behind a curtain, window-side gift, dreamlike small animal.

Visual elements:

- Half-open gift box, treasure box, inner drawer, ribbon, petals, glowing lining, tiny scene inside a teacup, curtain gap, starry envelope, roses, pearl candy, transparent cover.

Scene and material direction:

- Use inside/outside contrast, partial opening, layered packaging, warm inner glow, delicate contents, soft shadow around the closed part, and a clear focal reveal.

Usage principle:

- The image should show discovery: something wrapped, hidden, or covered is just being revealed. The surprise must come from the story object, not from generic sparkles.

Avoid:

- Piling hearts, roses, and glitter without a concrete surprise source.
- Using bright particles as a replacement for an actual reveal structure.
