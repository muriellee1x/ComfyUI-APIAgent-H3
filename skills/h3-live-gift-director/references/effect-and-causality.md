# Effect restraint and causal plausibility

## Zero-effect default

Begin every inferred design with no added magical or graphic effects. Add an effect only when at least one condition is true:

- it is visibly present in the reference;
- the user explicitly requests it;
- the named gift or story depends on a supernatural mechanism;
- it is required to make an otherwise invisible state change readable.

Do not add effects simply to make a gift feel expensive. Camera quality, staging, animation mechanics, material response, composition, and emotional timing carry value without glow trails.

## Effect necessity test

Before adding any glow, trail, ripple, sweep, particle path, or activation beam, answer:

1. What visible source produces it?
2. What does it physically or narratively affect?
3. Why can ordinary action, light, wind, water, or editing not communicate the same event?

If any answer is vague, omit the effect. Never write generic connectors such as a glint that races forward, a luminous wake that activates a distant object, or a magic ripple that illuminates the whole environment unless the user asked for that exact logic.

## Guiding-object gate

Do not invent a crystal, orb, petal messenger, musical note, star, butterfly, beam, or similar guide by default. A guiding object is allowed only when it already exists in the reference, is named by the user, or behaves as a plausible physical object central to the story. Its destination and influence must be immediately understandable.

Prefer these alternatives:

- let the actual character notice or approach the destination;
- let a vehicle physically carry the camera into the reveal;
- let wind, gravity, waves, or mechanical motion create the action;
- use an editorial cut to reveal a new location or result;
- let an existing object move, open, arrive, bloom, rotate, or settle without magical activation.

## Effect budget

Within 1000-3000 coins, zero effects is valid at every price. When effects are justified, use the minimum needed:

- one brief local accent for a compact gift;
- one supporting layer or one restrained propagation for a mid-range story;
- at most two coordinated layers for a richer 2200-3000-coin gift, unless explicitly requested otherwise.

Do not automatically combine trails, particles, light sweeps, ripples, rainbow refraction, and environmental illumination in the same prompt. Atmospheric snow, petals, sea spray, reflections, dust, and sunlight remain ordinary scene behavior unless described as supernatural.

## Physical examples

- A couple walks through snow because they are exploring, not because an invented crystal draws a glowing road.
- A sailboat passes an island and its bow spray bridges into a new wide view where the island attraction is already operating; the boat does not fire light at it.
- A woman walks through a flower street as wind moves roses and fabric; the pavement does not need a magic ring.
- A train opens its doors and balloons emerge through pressure and buoyancy; this is a coherent gift event and needs no additional activation beam.
