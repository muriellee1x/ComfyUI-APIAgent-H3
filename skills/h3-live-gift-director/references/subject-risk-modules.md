# Subject risk modules

Load only the modules present in the request. Treat every `Avoid` line below as an internal review checklist, not text for the generated H3 prompt. In the prompt, express controls as positive visible states: exact subject count, stable attachment, five separated fingers, aligned reflections, full-color ending, and continuing motion. Never append a negative-prompt sentence or enumerate unwanted artifacts.

## People and identity

Preserve face, hairstyle, headwear, costume construction, material, body proportions, left-right placement, and existing accessories. Keep expressions restrained and readable. Avoid identity drift, duplicated characters, clothing mutation, limb fusion, face replacement, and body penetration.

## Hands and contact

Specify which hand acts, where it enters, palm orientation, finger sequence, contact point, grip response, and release. Require exactly five naturally separated fingers. Avoid fused or extra fingers, intersecting palms, reversed wrists, and contact without weight response.

## Couples and gentle interaction

Define who initiates, who responds, body distance, contact order, and final posture. Keep support anatomically plausible. Avoid event-order reversal, abrupt intimacy, fused torsos, crossed limbs, and unsafe placement near edges.

## Animals

Preserve species, age, coat or feathers, proportions, head-tail orientation, appendages, and non-anthropomorphic behavior. Describe weight transfer and gait phase: lift, swing, contact, support, and follow-through. Avoid floating hooves, duplicated legs, gait reset across cuts, pouncing when approaching gently, or exaggerated human gestures.

## Antlers, horns, wings, tails, and ribbons

Preserve branch count, attachment, symmetry or intended asymmetry, size, material, and delayed secondary motion. Keep ribbons clear of legs and faces. Avoid detached wings, changing antler branches, tail-head swaps, clipping, and uncontrolled flapping.

## Vehicles and trains

Define the unique front, travel direction, route, wheel direction, steering behavior, carriage order, and camera-relative speed. Background motion must prove continued travel when the camera matches speed. Avoid duplicated vehicles, reversing, front/rear swaps, overtaking carriages, wheel reversal, derailment, drift unless requested, and stopping for a product pose.

## Amusement rides and mechanical facilities

Preserve structure, support geometry, spoke or track layout, cabin count logic, colors, and connections. Cabins remain upright under gravity; rides accelerate mechanically rather than starting at full speed. Avoid structural redesign, duplicated slides or tracks, inverted cabins, detached components, and camera penetration.

## Musical instruments

Preserve instrument orientation and anatomy. For guitar, place the fretting hand on the fingerboard and the strumming hand across the strings near the sound hole or pickups; describe chord change, stroke direction, string vibration, and resonance or decay. Avoid hovering hands, reversed holding, string multiplication, deformed necks, and premature stopping.

## Growth and assembly

Build in structural order. For trees: extend trunks upward while thickening the base, then primary branches, secondary branches, fine twigs, inner leaves, middle leaves, and outer leaves. For objects: base, supports, container or body, internal material, decoration, and settling. Avoid uniform scale-up, duplicate parts, melting, explosive concealment, and attachment without alignment.

## Dimensional transformation

For 3D-to-2D, compress depth progressively from the contact point, align the outline, and attach the planar result. Preserve anatomy until flattening completes. Avoid melting, stretching, fragmentation, explosion, and a duplicate 3D subject remaining afterward.

## Particles, petals, snow, and dust

Give particles a source, force, direction, density, depth, gravity behavior, and decay. Use them to reveal motion or cause a transition, not to hide weak mechanics. Avoid motionless suspension, reverse gravity, full-frame storms, and transformation into unrelated smoke.

## Fireworks

Use a physical sequence: launch, decelerate, burst, expand, fall, and fade. New fireworks may overlap old ones when continuous activity is required. Use ordinary circular, chrysanthemum, or willow forms unless explicitly requested. Avoid text, animals, hearts, special symbols, full-white frames, and smoke-only endings.

## Water and reflections

Maintain flow direction, ripple propagation, splash cause, buoyancy, and reflection alignment. Reflections distort with surface motion but do not become duplicate objects. Avoid frozen water, rigid light columns, reversed waterfalls, and objects moving independently of waves.

## Dynamic cover ending

Hold the final composition at stable exposure and full color while retaining appropriate motion through the last frame: breathing, ear turns, fabric, hair, foliage, water, reflections, particles, snow, lights, wheels, wings, smoke, or continued performance. Validate unwanted ending artifacts internally without naming them in the H3 prompt.
